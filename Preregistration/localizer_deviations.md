| 2026-11-28 | **Added exploratory behaviour-only check: can circle-tracking presses be recovered from the censored Extero / Match logs, and is the measure reliable?** *Correction to the registration's description (§1 "Responses"):* the responses were not lost entirely. In Extero and Match, each pacer half-cycle is one log row ("Trial"). It stores the **first** key within a response window that starts at the phase onset (`StimOnset`) and ends when the row is written: about 2.0 s in v2, and 0.8 × half-cycle in v1. The scanner trigger arrives as key `5` every 2 s on the same input. Across all logs, a real button is stored on about 22% of Extero / Match trials (95–96% of them the correct key for the phase), the trigger on about 69%, and nothing on about 9%. Self is essentially lost (4% real answers). The Biopac files have no button channel (all 67 hold CO2, belt and scanner pulse). A pre-check of three runs showed that `StimOnset` + RT of every stored `5` falls on a 2-s grid to within about 2 ms. So the time of the first trigger after each phase onset is known for every trial. *Measure (fixed now).* Per trial: a stored `1`/`2` is a press event at its RT. A stored `5`, or no key, is right-censored at the trial's censoring time C = min(first trigger after onset, end of window). The trigger's phase relative to the pacer is set by the scanner, so censoring is independent of behaviour, and Kaplan–Meier is unbiased under that assumption. The run's trigger phase φ is the median of (`StimOnset` − run start + RT) mod 2 over its stored `5`s. A run is excluded if the IQR of that quantity is > 0.02 s, or if computed and logged trigger latencies differ by a median > 0.01 s. **Primary score: F1 = KM probability of a press within 1.0 s of the phase onset.** **Secondary: RMST1.5** = restricted mean time to press over 0–1.5 s. Both are per participant × condition, from the attempt of record (`events_qc_full.csv`), on the registered run set (102 runs, 54 participants), with all Extero / Match trials with TrialNum > 0. Any button counts as a press.
